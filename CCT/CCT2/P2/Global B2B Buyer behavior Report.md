## 1. Executive Summary: The Evolving B2B Buyer Journey

### The Digital Transformation of B2B Purchasing

Over the last decade, buyer habits have changed drastically—and business-to-business (B2B) buyer habits are no exception. Buyers purchasing from suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and wholesalers are **no longer relying solely on sales teams** to guide their purchases.

>[!important] Key Trend: Hybrid Physical-Digital Journey
>The buyer journey is becoming a **mix of physical and digital interactions** that result in a final purchase online. B2B buyers are using online platforms in multiple ways throughout their purchasing process, not just for final transactions.

**B2B Online Platform Usage:**

| **Activity** | **Percentage** |
|---|---|
| Transact or purchase | 74% |
| Research or browse | 64% |
| Request a quote | 56% |

These aren't one-time interactions. Data shows buyers are purchasing online **more and more frequently**:

| **Frequency** | **Percentage** |
|---|---|
| **Everyday** | **15%** |
| **A few times per week** | **50%** |
| Once a week | 15% |
| Once every 2-3 weeks | 9% |
| Once a month | 6% |
| Every few months | 5% |

**Breakdown:**
- **65% purchase at least weekly** (15% daily + 50% few times/week)
- **80% purchase at least every 2-3 weeks** (adding 15% once/week)
- **High-frequency purchasing is the norm**, not the exception

>[!warning] Imperative for B2B Merchants
>"Buyers are turning to online channels to research, compare, and purchase products. B2B merchants need to have a strong online presence and offer a seamless omnichannel shopping experience to keep buyers loyal and converting, or risk losing market share."
>
>— Lance Owide, B2B General Manager, BigCommerce

### Methodology and Structure

**Survey Coverage:**
- B2B buyers across **United States (US), United Kingdom (UK), and Australia (AU)**
- Journey broken into three distinct categories:
  1. **Product Discovery**: How buyers find products
  2. **Shopping Experience**: What buyers value during evaluation
  3. **Checkout**: Payment preferences and friction points

### Summary Findings at a Glance

**Digital Adoption:**
- 74% use online platforms to purchase products
- 65% use search engines as main means of product discovery
- 42% cite online marketplaces (like Amazon Business)

**Social Proof and Marketplaces:**
- Vast majority influenced by customer ratings and reviews
- 60% use B2B marketplaces to make purchases
- 51% of marketplace users choose Amazon Business

**Pain Points:**
- **40%** inaccurate pricing and shipping costs
- **29%** slow website load times
- **28%** poor customer support
- **31%** technical issues prevented completing online purchase

**Checkout Priorities:**

Respondents indicated most important feature of checkout process was **not** mobile support or auto-renew functionality, but **displaying complete and accurate pricing information**.

## 2. Where B2B Buyers Find and Purchase Products

### 2.1. Product Discovery: Multi-Channel Approach

To understand buyer motivations and discovery process, the survey asked:
1. How do you find the products you buy (online or offline)?
2. What do you value most when looking at a product or listing?
3. What would motivate you to complete a purchase?

#### Search Engines and Marketplaces Lead, But Catalogs Persist

>[!info] Digital Platforms Dominate, Traditional Methods Survive
>While digital platforms like internet search results and online marketplaces prove to be the most frequently used ways buyers find products online, interestingly, **42% of global respondents discover products from catalogs**, signaling that catalogs are still a viable way of grabbing buyers' attention.

**Primary Product Discovery Channels:**

| **Channel** | **Global Percentage** |
|---|---|
| **Search engines** | **65%** |
| **Online marketplaces** | **42%** |
| **Catalogs (physical)** | **42%** |
| Sales representatives | (Not specified, but noted as valued) |
| Social media | Growing opportunity |

**Regional Variation:**

The outlier in this dataset is notably **online marketplaces**:
- **US**: Most reliant on online marketplaces for product discovery
- **AU**: Least reliant on online marketplaces for product discovery
- **UK**: (Mid-range, not specified)

>[!example] Multi-Channel Discovery in Practice
>A purchasing manager might:
>1. Receive a **physical catalog** highlighting new products (42% do this)
>2. **Search Google** for technical specifications and alternatives (65% do this)
>3. Check **Amazon Business** for pricing and reviews (42% do this)
>4. Contact **sales representative** for custom quote
>5. Make final purchase through supplier's website or marketplace

### 2.2. Purchasing Channels: Ecommerce Sites and Marketplaces Drive Sales

**Most Used Purchasing Channels (in order):**

1. **Supplier websites and apps** (selected by wide margin—most promising for businesses investing in ecommerce)
2. **B2B marketplaces**
3. **In-store/warehouse purchasing**

**Regional Differences in Marketplace Adoption:**

| **Region** | **B2B Marketplace Usage** |
|---|---|
| **US** | **65%** |
| **UK** | **62%** |
| **AU** | **45%** |

Australian buyers purchase **significantly less** through B2B marketplaces (45%) than counterparts in US (65%) and UK (62%).

#### Which Marketplace Do Buyers Prefer?

Among respondents who purchased on online marketplace, **which marketplace they used most**:

| **Marketplace** | **Global Percentage** |
|---|---|
| **Amazon Business** | **51%** |
| Walmart | 23% |
| Alibaba | 19% |

**Regional Marketplace Preferences:**

| **Marketplace** | **US** | **UK** | **AU** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Business** | **57%** | **58%** | **38%** |
| Walmart Business | **39%** | Low | Low |
| Alibaba | Lower | Lower | **21%** |

**Breakdown:**
- **Amazon Business dominates** US (57%) and UK (58%) but less so AU (38%)
- **Walmart Business** mainly selected by US respondents (39%)—unsurprising given Walmart's US focus
- **AU respondents** utilize Alibaba more (21%) to compensate for lower Amazon Business adoption

>[!tip] Marketplace Strategy Insight
>With 65% purchasing from marketplaces but only 50% finding products there, this could suggest buyers are **finding products through search or direct websites** and then heading to their favorite marketplaces to purchase.
>
>**Implication**: Merchants across industries should ensure presence on appropriate marketplaces (Amazon Business, Walmart, Alibaba, Rakuten depending on region/industry) even if discovery happens elsewhere.

### 2.3. Purchase Drivers: B2B Buyers Value Peer Opinions

**Major Drivers for Purchase Decision:**

1. **Ratings and reviews** (major driver)
2. **Peer recommendations** (major driver)
3. **Promotions and marketing** (ranked highly—importance of strong B2B marketing campaigns)
4. **Trade events** (further behind—still ramping up globally after pandemic halt)

>[!important] Social Proof in B2B
>Ratings and reviews, along with peer recommendations, are a **major driver** when it comes to making the decision to purchase. This parallels DTC (direct-to-consumer) behavior, showing B2B buyers bring consumer expectations to business purchasing.

Interestingly, **promotions and marketing ranked highly**, signaling importance of strong B2B marketing campaigns—not just sales outreach.

## 3. Key Takeaways: Product Discovery and Purchasing Channels

### Leverage Both Online and Offline Channels

>[!tip] Actionable Insight: Merge Online and Offline Marketing
>Even as online channels continue to play more of a role in B2B buying, respondents still showed preference for **physical media, such as product catalogs**.
>
>**Opportunity**: Merge online and offline marketing tactics by including links (via **QR code**, etc.) in physical marketing materials to ecommerce stores.

**Multi-Channel Strategy:**
- Prioritize **search engine optimization (SEO)** and **search engine marketing (SEM)**
- Recognize **social media channels** as growing opportunity to meet buyers at different journey points
- Don't abandon traditional methods: **42% still discover via catalogs**

**The Personal Touch Still Matters:**

Buyers still value personal interaction—**sales representatives remain critical** to sales process. Those who can effectively **arm sales teams with advantages of online channels** will have most success in coming years.

### Invest in Your Ecommerce Technology

>[!important] Investment Imperative
>With **74% of B2B buyers using ecommerce site to purchase products**, it is clear businesses should be investing in ecommerce technology that provides:
>- **Modern, user-centric experiences** for customers
>- **Improved internal operations** within sales and marketing teams

**The Cost of Legacy Systems:**

Remaining on decades-old legacy or bespoke platforms can prove **costly**. Now may be right time to start looking for alternatives.

>[!example] Technology Investment Areas
>1. **Customer-facing ecommerce platform**: Modern, mobile-responsive, fast-loading
>2. **Product Information Management (PIM)**: Manage large catalogs efficiently
>3. **Customer Relationship Management (CRM)**: Integrate sales team activities with online data
>4. **Marketing automation**: Personalized campaigns based on buyer behavior
>5. **Analytics and reporting**: Understand buyer journeys and optimize conversion

### List Your Products on Amazon Business

>[!tip] Critical Marketplace Presence
>With **65% purchasing from marketplaces**, but only **50% finding products there**, this suggests buyers find products through search or direct websites then head to favorite marketplaces to purchase.
>
>**One very important takeaway**: Merchants across industries should be **selling on Amazon Business**.

**Marketplace Selection by Region/Industry:**
- **US**: Amazon Business (57%), Walmart Business (39%)
- **UK**: Amazon Business (58%)
- **AU**: Amazon Business (38%), Alibaba (21%)
- **Other platforms**: Rakuten, regional marketplaces may prove worthwhile depending on industry

**Marketplace Strategy:**

Don't view marketplaces as competition to direct sales—view as **complementary channel** where buyers expect to find you after discovery elsewhere.

### Integrate More Customer Ratings and Reviews

>[!important] Social Proof as Conversion Driver
>Product pages have array of best practices (SEO, product imagery). For B2B merchants, beneficial to add **customer ratings and reviews** to that list.
>
>Buyers showed ratings and reviews were **major driver** for purchasing decisions.

**Implementation Strategy:**

1. **Encourage reviews**: Email buyers after purchase requesting reviews
2. **Monitor reviews**: Actively track and respond to all reviews (positive and negative)
3. **Communicate with customers**: Respond professionally no matter experience with product
4. **Consider third-party software**: High volumes may require review management platform

**Best Practices:**
- Make reviews prominent on product pages
- Allow filtering/sorting by rating
- Respond to negative reviews with solutions
- Highlight positive reviews in marketing materials
- Use review data to improve products and services

## 4. Why B2B Buyers Purchase Online vs. Other Channels

After understanding how buyers found products, survey asked:
- **Why** they purchased through online channels
- What experiences they valued most during shopping experience

**Question**: When thinking about purchasing products for your business, which statements **least and most** drive decision to shop via websites vs. other channels?

**Value Propositions Evaluated:**

1. Compare products and prices across competitors
2. Ability to customize orders, select specific products and quantities
3. Save purchase history, making reordering and tracking easy
4. Make purchases at any time from anywhere
5. Easier to know what total cost is going to be
6. Ability to see live inventory availability and out of stock
7. Receive customized suggestions based on search/purchasing history

### 4.1. B2B Buyers Are Feeling the Impacts of Inflation

>[!important] Pricing Sensitivity Dominates
>Pricing continues to be **sensitive area** for B2B buyers. Majority of respondents indicate that being able to **compare products and prices** drives their purchasing decision online when compared to other value propositions.

**Regional Variations:**

While some value propositions rated lower on average, regional breakdown tells different story:

| **Region** | **Top Priorities** |
|---|---|
| **US** | • Ability to purchase anytime, anywhere<br>• Compare products and prices |
| **UK** | • See total cost<br>• Live inventory visibility |
| **AU** | • Ability to purchase anytime, anywhere<br>• Compare products and prices |

**Breakdown:**
- **US and AU**: Much greater preference for **convenience** (anytime, anywhere purchasing)
- **UK**: Most interested in **cost transparency** and **inventory visibility**

This may reflect different business cultures, competitive landscapes, or supply chain concerns in each region.

### 4.2. Main Pain Points During Online Shopping

To understand potential pain points, survey asked respondents to rank experiences from **least to most frustrating**.

**Top Pain Points (Ranked):**

| **Rank** | **Pain Point** | **Percentage** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Inaccurate pricing and shipping costs** | **40%** |
| **2** | **Slow website loading times** | **29%** |
| **3** | **Poor customer support** | **28%** |

>[!warning] Pricing Transparency is Critical
>Inaccurate pricing and shipping costs deemed **most frustrating** by 40% of respondents.
>
>**Opportunity**: Merchants can improve pricing accuracy and transparency across sales channels to address #1 pain point.

**Other Key Pain Points:**
- **Slow website loading** (29%): Second-highest pain point—speed matters
- **Poor customer support** (28%): Following close behind—buyers need help when issues arise

These findings highlight that **basic ecommerce functionality** must work properly before advanced features matter.

### 4.3. User Experience Features B2B Buyers Value Most

**Ranked Features (Most to Least Important):**

1. **Detailed product information**
2. **Custom pricing and discounts**
3. **Account management**
4. **Efficient reordering**
5. **Bulk ordering**
6. **Advanced search and navigation**
7. **Customizable quotes**

#### Variations by Business Maturity

**Businesses >1 year old:**
- **Lower preference**: Advanced search and navigation (perhaps already know what they need)

**Businesses <1 year old:**
- **Higher preference**: Advanced search and navigation (still discovering products/vendors)

#### Variations by Purchase Frequency

**Lower Purchase Frequency (occasional buyers):**
- **Increasing preference**: Detailed product information (want more info to make confident decision when ready)

**Higher Purchase Frequency (frequent buyers):**
- **Higher preference**: Bulk ordering (efficiency matters for repeat purchases)

#### Variations by Approval Levels

**Multiple Levels of Approval:**
- **Higher preference**: Efficient reordering and account management
- **Reason**: Potential challenges when making new purchases; streamlining repeat purchases valuable

>[!example] Purchase Frequency Impact on Preferences
>**Occasional buyer** (purchases every few months):
>- Values: Detailed product info (need to re-educate self each time)
>- Values: Custom pricing (each purchase is significant decision)
>
>**Frequent buyer** (purchases few times per week):
>- Values: Bulk ordering (efficiency at scale)
>- Values: Efficient reordering (repeat purchases quickly)
>- Values: Saved purchase history (don't want to start from scratch)

## 5. Key Takeaways: Shopping Experience

### Focus on Basic Website Functionality to Alleviate Frustrations

>[!important] Get Basics Right First
>Merchants need to get **basics of website right** before moving on to bigger and better functionality.
>
>While there are specific features B2B buyers expect, many basic functions come from experiences in DTC space:
>- Accurate product and shipping information
>- Quick-loading website
>- Quality customer support

**Technology Partners Can Help:**

Look for potential partners to alleviate stresses:

**Customer Service:**
- Implement **live chat function** allowing buyers to speak directly to representative from site
- Explore **artificial intelligence (AI)** to reduce burden on customer support (chatbots for FAQs, ticket routing)

**Site Performance:**
- Use **content delivery network (CDN)** to improve load times globally
- Optimize images and code for speed
- Monitor site performance continuously

### Personalization: Not Yet a Priority

>[!note] Personalization Can Wait
>Respondents shared that **suggestions based on purchasing history were not driving factor** when it comes to why they shop via websites over other channels.
>
>**Inference**: By time of purchase, buyers **already know what they want**—looking for best price or most convenient way to shop.

**What This Means:**
- Doesn't mean buyers **not interested** in personalization
- Just that it's **not as much value add** as for DTC shoppers
- As more B2B merchants invest in personalization, could change over time

**Current Priorities:**

To align with B2B buyer priorities, merchants should **ensure basics of good website experience before tackling advanced features** like personalization.

**When Personalization Makes Sense:**
- After basics are solid
- For frequent buyers (suggest reorder items)
- For complementary products (based on past purchases)
- For account-specific pricing (showing contract rates automatically)

### Offer Robust Product Information to Help Buyers Make Decisions

>[!tip] Product Information is Critical for All Ecommerce
>Whether selling B2B or DTC, product information is **vital for ecommerce success**:
>- Technical specifications
>- High-quality imagery
>- Customer reviews
>- Real-time inventory

**Data Shows:**

B2B buyers across regions **value and reward robust and accurate product information**, especially as purchasing frequency decreases (occasional buyers need more info to decide).

**Managing Product Information at Scale:**

To manage product information for larger catalogs, merchants may benefit from investing in **Product Information Management (PIM) system** to help manage large quantities of product data.

>[!example] PIM System Benefits
>**Without PIM**:
>- Product data scattered across spreadsheets, databases
>- Inconsistent information across channels
>- Manual updates time-consuming, error-prone
>
>**With PIM**:
>- Centralized product data repository
>- Consistent information across website, marketplaces, catalogs
>- Automated syndication to multiple channels
>- Easier to maintain accuracy and completeness

### Enable Seamless Reordering and Modification

>[!important] Future Necessity: Reordering Features
>"Surfacing purchasing features that allow buyers to easily reorder or modify past orders or shopping lists is going to become a **necessity** for suppliers to continue providing seamless purchasing experience for their buyers."
>
>— Alec Berkley, Director of Business Development, BigCommerce

**Reordering Features to Implement:**
- **Order history**: Easily accessible, searchable past orders
- **Quick reorder button**: One-click to add past order to cart
- **Shopping lists**: Save frequently ordered items
- **Subscription/auto-replenishment**: Automatic recurring orders
- **Modify and reorder**: Adjust quantities before reordering

This becomes especially important for buyers with multiple approval levels who want to streamline repeat purchases.

## 6. The Checkout Process: Meeting B2B Buyer Expectations

Checkout process is arguably **most important step** in ecommerce. Even slightest bit of friction can cause buyer to abandon cart.

**Questions Asked:**
1. How do you pay for products?
2. What pain points do you experience during checkout process?
3. How do those pain points impact your final purchase?

### 6.1. B2B Buyers' Most-Used Payment Methods

**Primary Payment Methods (by popularity):**

1. **Credit and debit cards** (most used across all regions)
2. **Automated Clearing House (ACH)** (traditional B2B method)
3. **Purchase Order (PO) invoicing** (traditional B2B method)

**Regional Variations:**

Interesting outliers in dataset:

**US:**
- **Electronic checks**: 21%
- **Physical checks**: 18%

This may be because buyers in US show **more propensity to purchase in-store or at warehouse** than counterparts in UK and AU, where checks more commonly used.

>[!note] Traditional B2B Payment Methods Persist
>While credit/debit cards dominate, traditional B2B payment methods (ACH, PO invoicing) follow closely behind. Merchants must support **multiple payment methods** to accommodate different buyer preferences and internal processes.

**Payment Method Strategy:**

**Must-have payment methods:**
- Credit/debit cards (universal expectation)
- ACH/bank transfer (for larger orders)
- PO invoicing (for established accounts)

**Region-specific considerations:**
- **US**: Support for checks (electronic and physical)
- **UK/AU**: Less emphasis on checks

**Advanced payment options:**
- Digital wallets (PayPal, Apple Pay)
- Buy now, pay later (BNPL) for B2B

### 6.3. Checkout Features B2B Buyers Value Most

#### Total Price Display: Overwhelming Priority

>[!important] Pricing Transparency Dominates Checkout Preferences
>Respondents **overwhelmingly value total and accurate pricing information** over other features.
>
>This reinforces aforementioned data showing B2B buyers most concerned with **pricing and shipping costs**.

**What "Total Price Display" Means:**
- Show product prices clearly
- Display shipping costs **before** final checkout step
- Include taxes/duties where applicable
- Show discounts/promotions applied
- Calculate total before requiring payment information

#### Features by Approval Levels

While not showing high preference overall, respondents with **higher levels of approvals (10+ approvals)** showed some preference for:

**Auto-save cart feature:**
- **Why**: Buyers making slower purchase decisions
- **Why**: Need to come back to cart later during approval process

**Auto-renew or subscribe-and-save options:**
- **Why**: These features help reduce time approval process takes
- **Why**: Particularly if same order/purchase approved before (precedent set)

>[!example] High-Approval Purchase Journey
>Buyer at large enterprise with 10+ approval levels:
>
>**Day 1**: Researches products, adds to cart
>**Day 2**: Submits cart to first-level approver (needs **auto-save cart**)
>**Week 1-2**: Cart goes through approval chain
>**Week 3**: Final approval received, purchase made
>
>**Next quarter**: Same order needed again
>- **With auto-renew**: Automatically approved based on precedent, ships immediately
>- **Without auto-renew**: Entire approval process repeated
>
>**Time saved**: 2-3 weeks per recurring order

## 7. Key Takeaways: Checkout Process

### Keep Your Checkout Fast, Simple, and Secure

>[!important] Basic Functionality is Non-Negotiable
>Buyers expect basic functionality from vendor's website—**especially when it comes to checkout**.
>
>As ecommerce evolves, lines between B2B and DTC are blurring. Buyers bring expectations from DTC space and expect same from B2B merchants:
>- Fast load times
>- Working links
>- Multiple payment methods
>- And more

**The Competitive Imperative:**

If you're not meeting buyers' most basic needs, chances are they **can—and will—get better experience elsewhere**.

**Complicated Checkout Drives Buyers to Competitors:**

By keeping checkout limited to **single page**, or allowing buyers to **save carts and return later**, merchants can:
- Remove unnecessary friction from buying process
- Keep buyers happy at every stage of journey

>[!example] Single-Page Checkout Best Practices
>**Traditional multi-page checkout** (5 pages):
>1. Cart review
>2. Shipping address
>3. Shipping method
>4. Payment information
>5. Order review
>
>**Problem**: 5 opportunities for buyer to abandon, 5 page loads
>
>**Single-page checkout**:
>- All steps visible on one page
>- Progress indicators show completion
>- Easy to review entire order before submitting
>- Fewer page loads = less abandonment

### Security Must Be Visible and Robust

Although checkout security was **main concern of UK respondents (30%)**, lack of secure checkout process is still concern for buyers in:
- **US**: 20%
- **AU**: 21%

>[!warning] Security is Universal Concern
>Merchants **must be able to protect buyers' sensitive information**. Once buyers feel sense of security, they will **reward merchants with purchases**.

**Security Best Practices:**

**Technical Security:**
- **SSL certificate** (HTTPS, not HTTP)
- **PCI-compliant payment processing** (don't store card data directly)
- **Tokenization** of payment information
- **Fraud detection** systems
- **Secure authentication** (passwords, 2FA for account access)

**Trust Signals:**
- Display security badges (Norton, McAfee, SSL certificate logos)
- Show compliance certifications (PCI DSS, ISO standards)
- Provide clear privacy policy
- Highlight secure payment partners (Visa, Mastercard, PayPal logos)
- Include customer testimonials about safe transactions

## 8. Three Major Trends in B2B Buyer Expectations

After surveying buyers across US, UK, and AU, research shows **three major trends** in B2B buyer expectations, each driven by continued **blurring of lines between DTC and B2B ecommerce**.

### Trend 1: Buyers Expect—and Reward—Basic Online Functionality

>[!important] Baseline Expectations Have Risen
>Respondents indicated that **most basic ecommerce functionality** is extremely important when it comes to shopping experience:
>- Accurate product and pricing information
>- Autosaving carts
>- Fast page loads
>- Working checkout
>
>Majority of respondents indicated **fast, frictionless experiences** were preferred.

**Especially True For:**

Buyers with **multiple levels of approval**, which is common in B2B space.

**Abandonment Drivers:**
- **Technical issues** can cause buyers to abandon carts
- **Questionable checkout security** can cause buyers to abandon carts

This underscores importance of **basic ecommerce features and functionality**—must work reliably before advanced features matter.

>[!example] Basic Functionality Checklist
>**Before investing in AI personalization or advanced analytics**, ensure these basics work:
>
>✅ **Site Performance**:
>- Pages load in <3 seconds
>- Mobile-responsive design
>- No broken links or images
>
>✅ **Product Information**:
>- Complete, accurate descriptions
>- High-quality images
>- Real-time inventory display
>- Customer reviews visible
>
>✅ **Checkout**:
>- Single-page or streamlined multi-page
>- All payment methods work correctly
>- Clear error messages
>- Cart saves automatically
>
>✅ **Security**:
>- SSL certificate active
>- Trust badges displayed
>- PCI-compliant payment processing
>
>✅ **Customer Support**:
>- Contact information easy to find
>- Live chat available (or clear response time)
>- FAQ/help section comprehensive

### Trend 2: Buyers Want More Transparency When It Comes to Products and Pricing

>[!important] Transparency Builds Trust and Drives Conversion
>B2B buyers want to see **prices and product information displayed clearly and transparently** on ecommerce websites.
>
>Indicated by:
>- Value proposition preference
>- User experience feature preference: **Total price display**

**Economic Context:**

No matter what economic factors in play, important to remember **price will be at front of shoppers' minds**—especially during inflationary periods.

**Common Mistake:**

Merchants may unintentionally **hide shipping costs or taxes until final stages of checkout**. This creates frustration and abandonment.

>[!tip] Pricing Transparency Best Practices
>**Be clear and transparent** about price of your products:
>
>1. **Show full price early**: Display all costs (product + shipping + taxes) as soon as possible
>2. **Integrate shipping calculator**: Allow customers to see shipping costs before adding to cart
>3. **Display contract pricing**: If customer has special pricing, show it on product pages
>4. **Highlight discounts**: Make promotions and volume discounts obvious
>5. **Avoid surprise fees**: No hidden charges at checkout

**Shipping Tool Integration:**

Integrate shipping tool that allows customers to see **full price of goods before deciding to make purchase**:
- Enter ZIP/postal code on product page
- See estimated shipping cost before adding to cart
- No surprises at checkout

**Valuable Takeaway:**

Particularly important for merchants who may unintentionally hide costs. Transparency reduces abandonment and builds buyer trust.

### Trend 3: Buyers Expect a Frictionless Checkout Experience

>[!important] DTC Expectations Spill Over Into B2B
>Buyers' ecommerce experiences from **personal DTC purchases are spilling over** into buying they perform at work.
>
>While certain B2B-specific functions necessary (PO invoicing, ACH, bulk ordering, etc.), B2B merchants need to provide **DTC-like functionality where possible**.

**DTC-Like Functionality Buyers Expect:**

1. **Single-page checkout**: Minimize steps and page loads
2. **Mobile optimization**: Checkout works seamlessly on mobile devices
3. **Uncomplicated checkout process**: Clear, intuitive flow
4. **Security**: Visible trust signals and robust protection

**Balancing B2B and DTC Needs:**

| **B2B-Specific (Must Have)** | **DTC-Like (Also Expected)** |
|---|---|
| PO invoicing | Single-page checkout |
| ACH/bank transfer | Mobile optimization |
| Net terms | Fast load times |
| Approval workflows | Autosave cart |
| Bulk ordering | Clear progress indicators |
| Account-specific pricing | Guest checkout option (for small orders) |

>[!example] Frictionless B2B Checkout in Practice
>**Traditional B2B checkout** (high friction):
>- Requires account creation before browsing
>- Multi-step checkout (5+ pages)
>- Limited payment methods (PO only)
>- No cart save feature
>- Desktop-only (doesn't work on mobile)
>- No price visibility until checkout
>
>**Modern B2B checkout** (low friction):
>- Guest browsing (account optional)
>- Single-page checkout
>- Multiple payment methods (card, ACH, PO, net terms)
>- Auto-save cart
>- Mobile-optimized
>- Full price visibility before checkout
>- **Plus B2B features**: Bulk order upload, approval workflows, contract pricing

**The Importance of Modern Platforms:**

As B2B buyer expectations evolve, important for merchants to **meet—and even exceed—them** to succeed.

Also important for merchants to use **ecommerce platform that provides**:
- Solid foundation for basic functionality
- Framework for future growth
- Ability to add B2B-specific features without sacrificing DTC-like experience
